Why insurers should look again at their outsourcing contracts before March 2027

The PRA's updated outsourcing and third-party statement takes effect on 18 March 2027. It is a good moment to check that the work done in 2022 still holds.

The Prudential Regulation Authority's supervisory statement on outsourcing and third party risk management, SS2/21, first took effect on 31 March 2022. It sets out how PRA-regulated firms, including Solvency II insurers and reinsurers, are expected to manage their outsourcing and other third-party arrangements. An updated version was published on 18 March 2026 alongside new rules on operational incident and third-party reporting, and it takes effect on 18 March 2027.

For many insurers, the original work to meet SS2/21 was done as a project in 2021 and 2022. The contracts were reviewed, a register was built and the policy was approved. Since then, suppliers have changed, contracts have renewed and the register may not have kept up. The 2027 update is a sensible moment to check whether that early work still holds.

What the statement expects

We would always recommend reading the statement itself, but several of its expectations are especially relevant to supplier contracts.

  • Firms should assess all of their third-party arrangements for materiality and risk, not only those that are formally classed as outsourcing.
  • Contracts for material arrangements should include appropriate rights of access, audit and information, and should deal with sub-outsourcing.
  • Firms should have business continuity and exit plans for material arrangements that are realistic, not just documented.

Where the gaps usually are

In our experience, the gaps are rarely in the policy. They tend to be in the day-to-day records and contracts.

  • The register is out of date. Suppliers added since the original project may never have been assessed for materiality.
  • Older contracts were never fully remediated. Some agreements signed before the statement took effect may still lack the access, audit or sub-outsourcing terms it expects.
  • Exit plans exist only on paper. A plan written once and never tested may not survive contact with a real exit.
  • Ownership is unclear. Responsibility sits somewhere between procurement, risk, IT and the business, and nobody holds the full picture.

A practical review

A proportionate review does not need to be a large project. Start with the arrangements you consider material. For each one, confirm that the register entry is accurate, that the contract contains the terms the statement expects, and that the exit plan has been reviewed recently. Then check the register for suppliers that were added after the original exercise and have never been assessed.
