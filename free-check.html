02 The 14 recovery levers

Where value leaks.

Every contract is tested against the same fourteen levers. Each finding is tied to an invoice line or a clause.

  1. 01

    Unwanted services

    Services still billed that you no longer need or asked to stop.

  2. 02

    Oversold scope

    Capacity, tiers or service levels sold beyond what you use.

  3. 03

    Unused licences

    Licences assigned and paid for that nobody uses.

  4. 04

    Duplicate services

    The same capability bought twice, from one supplier or two.

  5. 05

    Stranded services

    Charges for sites, assets or users that have gone.

  6. 06

    Unclaimed credits and rebates

    Service credits and rebates the contract gives you, never claimed.

  7. 07

    Price and indexation errors

    Rates and annual uplifts that don't match the contract.

  8. 08

    Unsupported charges

    Invoice lines with no basis in the contract or an agreed change.

  9. 09

    Renewal traps

    Auto-renewals and notice periods that lock in cost.

  10. 10

    Undrawn entitlements

    Training, service hours or funding paid for but not used.

  11. 11

    Volume and tier misalignment

    Price bands that no longer reflect your real volumes.

  12. 12

    Terms to fix at renewal

    Weak clauses to put right before you sign again.

  13. 13

    Wrong pricing mechanism

    A fixed price you can't reduce as use falls, or open-ended time and materials for steady work.

  14. 14

    Exit and termination exposure

    Charges, lock-in or missing exit terms that make it costly to leave or cut scope.

03 Contract fitness

Is the contract still fit for purpose?

Finding leakage is half the job. We also test whether the deal itself still works for your business as it is today.

Pricing mechanism

Fixed price, time and materials or per unit: does the way you pay still match how much you use?

Exit and termination

Can you leave, or remove one service, without a penalty? Is there a current, priced exit plan?

Performance

Do the service levels measure what matters, and are service credits actually paid?

Scope

Do the services still match what your business needs today?

Change and flexibility

Can you add or remove services at known prices, without being locked in?

Governance

Are governance meetings, reporting and audit rights actually working?

Risk and compliance

Liability, data protection and regulation, including PRA SS2/21 for financial services.

Our recommendation

Keep, fix at renewal, renegotiate now, re-source or plan exit, with the reasons and the outcomes we can deliver.

04 Measuring value

How we measure value.

We agree the baseline with finance first. Then we report each kind of value separately, so nothing is double-counted.

One baseline. Three kinds of value.

Baseline agreed with finance

What you pay today, agreed with your finance team before we start.

Hard savings

A lower recurring cost against the baseline.

Cost avoidance

Increases that would have happened, and now won't.

One-off recoveries

Credits, refunds and rebates recovered once.

Confidence levels: from Identified to Realised.

Every finding carries a confidence level, so you always know how firm a number is.

  1. Identified

    We have found a likely issue in the invoices or the contract.

  2. Validated

    The evidence is checked against invoice lines and contract clauses, and the service owner confirms it.

  3. Agreed

    You approve the action and your finance team accepts the value.

  4. Realised

    The saving or recovery shows in your invoices, confirmed by finance.
