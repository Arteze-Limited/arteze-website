When an organisation changes, its supplier contracts rarely change at the same pace. A site closes, two plants merge, a system is replaced or a team is restructured. The work stops, but the invoices carry on. Retired applications keep their support charges, leavers keep their software seats, and network circuits at buildings nobody uses are still billed every month.

This happens for a simple reason. The people who make the operational change are rarely the people who manage the contracts or approve the invoices. The project team decommissions the system and moves on. Nobody tells the contract owner, and the supplier has no reason to point it out. In manufacturing, where site changes and system consolidations are common, this can go unnoticed for years.

### Where to look first

The same few categories account for most of the waste.

- **Telecoms and network services**, especially circuits, lines and mobile contracts tied to sites that have closed or moved.
- **Software licences and subscriptions**, including seats assigned to people who have left and add-on modules nobody uses.
- **Support and maintenance**, for hardware or applications that have been retired or replaced.
- **Managed services** priced on volumes, such as devices, users or sites, that are now lower than the contract assumes.
- **Facilities services**, such as cleaning, security and waste collection, at sites that are no longer occupied.

### A simple way to start

Take a list of every site, system and major team change in the last two years. For each one, ask which suppliers were involved in supporting it, and check whether their invoices changed afterwards. Where they did not, ask the supplier to confirm what is still being provided.

It also helps to look at it from the other direction. Pick ten recurring charges from your largest invoices and ask the budget holder what each one is for. If nobody can explain a charge with confidence, it is worth investigating.

### Stopping it happening again

The lasting fix is to link change to contracts. Any project that closes a site, retires a system or reduces headcount should include a step to review the affected supplier contracts, with a named owner. It is a small addition to a project plan, and it stops the problem at the source.

Recovering money already paid is sometimes possible, depending on the contract terms and how quickly the issue is raised.
