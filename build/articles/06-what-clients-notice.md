Our founder, Mahi, spent more than 25 years on the buyer's side of the table, managing suppliers in banking, energy, rail and the public sector. In that time he sat through a great many service reviews and renewal meetings. Some of the most important things a client thinks about its supplier are almost never said in those meetings, because there is rarely a comfortable moment to say them.

These are some of the things clients notice.

### Who turns up, and when

Clients notice when the provider's senior people appear only at renewal time. If the client partner was absent for two years and then arrives with a proposal, the client reads that as a statement about how the provider values the account. Steady, modest senior attention between contract events counts for far more than a strong showing at the end.

### Whether the team changed after the contract was signed

The people who win a contract are often not the people who deliver it. Clients accept that some change is normal. What they remember is when the experienced team that led the bid disappears within months of go-live and the knowledge goes with them.

### How change requests were priced

A client may never complain about the price of a single change request. But they keep a mental tally. If small changes were consistently quoted high and slowly, that tally shapes how they feel at renewal, regardless of what the service reports say.

### Whether problems were raised early

Clients forgive incidents far more readily than surprises. A provider that raises a risk early, explains it plainly and proposes a fix earns more trust than one whose reports always look perfect. When a client later discovers a problem that the provider knew about and did not mention, trust drops quickly and takes a long time to recover.

### How the renewal proposal arrived

A proposal that arrives late, with little time to review it, tells the client the provider is relying on the cost of switching rather than on the value of the service. Most clients notice that, even when they renew.

### Why this matters to you

None of these points appear on an SLA dashboard, and most clients will not raise them directly. That does not mean they are minor. Taken together, they often decide whether a client renews, reduces scope or goes to market.
